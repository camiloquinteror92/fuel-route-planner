      Why it is fast

      • No geocoding call for “City, ST”. “lat,lon” is parsed and “City, ST” is looked up in an offline index of — places (Census and GeoNames); only text the index cannot place goes to Nominatim, and that answer is cached.
      • One routing call, small. OSRM answers in polyline6, decoded locally, over an HTTPS connection that stays open between trips.
      • Two caches and single flight. The prepared route and the finished plan are cached apart (route for —, plan for —, per server process), so many what-ifs never push the route out; identical requests in flight share one routing call.
      • A what-if costs no call. The route is cached apart from the plan, so a change of the truck or of the rules in What if… is planned again on the cached route by our code alone.
      • Vectorised corridor search. numpy finds the stations near the route in three passes: see the funnel in How it works.
      • Gzip on the wire. The JSON of a long trip and this page compress to a fraction of their size; the “Transfer” card at the top of this tab compares what travelled with what was decoded.
